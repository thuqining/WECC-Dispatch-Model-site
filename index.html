Each day it clears CAISO (four zones on PowerWorld PTDFs) and the other 35 EIA-930 balancing authorities (links limited hour by hour around their EIA-930 flow) as two markets: day-ahead unit commitment over 3,695 EIA-860M plant groups, then 5-minute real time. California offers are CAISO's published bids. CAISO's imports and exports are not fixed to history: a price-taking block predicted from day-ahead information (load, solar, wind and gas forecasts) plus economic import offers and export bids per corridor (north at NP15, south at SCE) at the neighbours' model price of the day before plus cap-and-trade cost ($12.8) and a $3 hurdle, cleared under CAISO's intertie rules and held at their day-ahead schedule in real time. Reserves by region: CAISO regulation up 1 % / down 2 % / contingency 6 % (at least 1,150 MW); the rest of WECC 3 % / 3 % / 6 % (BAL-002-WECC). Headline metric: CAISO's three DLAPs only. How the model is judged. Only CAISO's own published prices count: the day-ahead and real-time LMPs of its three default load aggregation points (DLAP PG&E, SCE, SDG&E). Those are the headline numbers on this page and the only ones used to choose the model. Prices shown for every other area are the model's own shadow prices (the dual of each area's energy balance) set against CAISO's published WEIM ELAP prices; they are shown for comparison and must not be far off, but they are indicative only and not in the headline metrics.

California's offers are CAISO's own published day-ahead bids at the same position in the merit order, so the height of California's tail is data, not a model choice. Outside California no bids are published and blocks offer at 1.1 x variable cost. Wind and solar count at their best hour; their negative offers are CAISO's published renewable bid steps, so capacity below $0 is what the market has to pay to curtail.

2025, a year the model never saw model2 run unchanged over all 53 weeks of 2025 and scored against CAISO's three DLAPs; every one of those weeks is also in the model selector, with full 5-minute detail in every other view

Each week was solved on its own with one warm-up day, exactly as the 2024 study weeks were. The model is given what actually happened — load, wind, solar, hydro and interchange between the other balancing authorities from EIA-930 (CAISO's own imports and exports are a predicted price-taking block plus economic interties cleared under CAISO's rules) (California's load, wind and solar from CAISO's OASIS, its load net of battery charging), the plant fleet from EIA-860M (July 2025), California's thermal, storage, solar and wind offers from CAISO's published bids for that date and hour, gas from CAISO's daily fuel index, and each week's starting battery charge from CAISO's storage report — so this reproduces prices under known conditions rather than forecasting them. Calibrated rather than observed: 90 % of the battery fleet available, the 900 MW south-to-north limit on Path 15, the balancing-authority link limits (each hour 1.25× the link's EIA-930 flow plus 300 MW, within 1.5× its 1st/99th percentile flow), WEIM trade with CAISO within each tie's remaining hourly capacity (its 1st/99th percentile flow less the historical flow), energy-neutral per day, thermal offers outside California at half their cost plus half CAISO's hourly price, Northwest hydro within ±10 % of actual, with a $2 greenhouse-gas wedge on sales into California, and hydro outside California offering 1.4× the day's mean CAISO day-ahead price within ±20 % of its actual hourly output. Coal outside California is held within 90-100 % of its EPA CEMS output, which carries its outages; other generator and transmission outages are not modelled. Cards compare with R38, the previous model, scored the same way.

Midday is 10:00-16:00 (UTC−6). California's three areas: MAE 11.4, correlation 0.57. The other twelve: MAE 11.3, correlation 0.36; negative prices in 5.2 % of intervals, against 7.5 % at their ELAPs (midday 13.1 % against 18.4 %). Across those twelve areas CAISO's prices differ by a median of $15.2 at the same interval; the model's by $2.06. Why the gap remains: model2 solves the rest of the West as 35 EIA-930 balancing authorities joined by transport links with no AC network; each link's limit follows its EIA-930 flow hour by hour (1.25x the flow plus 300 MW in its direction, 300 MW against it), which took the median price range across these areas from $1.9 to its present value. CAISO's ELAP prices also differ by its own congestion and loss components and by WEIM transfer limits that EIA-930 does not publish, so the model only separates areas when a link limit binds. Where the price is driven by California (the Southwest's midday surplus, LADWP, Nevada) the negatives are reproduced; in New Mexico, El Paso and the Rockies they are not.
